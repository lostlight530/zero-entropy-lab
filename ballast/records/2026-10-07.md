## 研究摘要

This Daily tests whether observing a mutating admission webhook once or again under reinvocation can be promoted into proof of the final admitted object or task completion.

Kubernetes explicitly does not guarantee a fixed additional invocation count or stable mutating-webhook order. Reinvocation can help a webhook observe later mutations, but final-state policy belongs at a validating boundary and idempotence remains required.

No live Kubernetes mutation or external non-idempotent effect is executed.

## 研究问题

Can initial invocation or reinvocation evidence prove the final object and valid completion when later mutations, non-idempotent behavior, authority drift and target reincarnation remain possible.

## 来源依据

Checked 2026-10-07 against current Kubernetes documentation.

[Kubernetes Dynamic Admission Control](https://kubernetes.io/docs/reference/access-authn-authz/extensible-admission-controllers/) states that additional reinvocations are not guaranteed to be exactly one, ordering can change, and a webhook is not guaranteed to be invoked again after every later modification.

[Kubernetes Admission Webhook Good Practices](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/cluster-administration/admission-webhooks-good-practices/) recommends not relying on mutating webhook order, requires idempotent mutation behavior, and recommends validating the final state.

These sources constrain admission semantics only. They do not prove this bounded fixture or a live cluster result.

## 可证伪假设

H1 observing the initial mutating webhook invocation is sufficient to prove final-object state.

H2 observing reinvocation is sufficient to prove that no later mutation can invalidate the task predicate.

H3 final-object validation alone proves valid task completion even when current permission, prior effect and incarnation evidence are unresolved.

H4 separating invocation evidence, final-object validation, current permission, prior-effect evidence, target incarnation and task-defined completion rejects the modeled false completions.

## 路径比较

invocation-only collapses an intermediate admission observation into final state.

reinvocation-aware improves visibility but still assumes the observed reinvocation is a terminal boundary.

final-object-validation correctly rejects final-state invalidity and missing final evidence, but still cannot prove permission, prior-effect history, exact incarnation or broader task completion.

full-integrity has zero disagreement in this bounded fixture because it keeps these axes separate.

## 暂时结论

Reinvocation is an observation opportunity, not a completion receipt.

Final-object validation is stronger than mutating-webhook observation but still does not collapse current permission, historical effect evidence, target incarnation or task-defined completion.

H4 is supported only by this bounded model.

## AGI-scale action-integrity relevance

Long-running agents often treat middleware callbacks, hooks and retries as checkpoints. A callback observed twice can still be an intermediate state rather than a protected completion boundary.

Harnesses should bind completion to the final state and the exact authority, identity and effect-set contract that the task requires.
